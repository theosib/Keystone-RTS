I had two different AI models develop two different game concepts from essentially the same prompt. There's a lot of overlap, some probably due to the prompt and some due to general genre of game. But we need to be careful that the shared ideas don't look too much like ripoffs from a common ancestor. At least not in the legal sense. There's an extent to which a familiar idea (the AoE concept) done in a new way (using a voxel engine) could be a real draw to the player. Familiar without being a ripoff and a creative new direction are difficult to balance.

We need to have some serious AI built into this game. One level is individual workers who are entities that have jobs and needs and must navigate the world to do those jobs. Another level is cooperating and/or competing civilizations in a single player games (as well as extra players in multiplayer), where high-level strategy is employed (at a few different difficulty levels) to advance their own civilizations, so that AI needs be able emulate other players, and emulate them faithfully to how a human would play, without cheating (except maybe an ultra-hard level). We also all have fog of war.

I really like the proposal to have physical stockpiles. With every resource stored in the settlement, we're going to have a storage problem. So actually, some AI should be spent on keeping it organized so that workers can find the raw materials they need. So resources get stored as blocks instead of items in containers. A given block can represent up to some maximum amount of a resource (appearing different for different amounts), and then workers have to manage lots of blocks. Workers also have to be able to get to those blocks, and one way to do that would be for workers to build floors and build upwards as well as outwards.

For game engine generality: Support multiple languages in the UI.

As part of the copyright/trademark avoidance, we also need to be careful about our textures and blocks looking too much like an existing game. We can borrow high-level concepts, not game assets.

Advanced idea that doesn't necessarily fit into version 1: I think enemy vs ally need not be fixed. While some players may prefer to know in advance who is on their side and who is not, others may prefer to negotiate treaties and demonstrate their allegiance by offering tributes, going on the attack, etc. But the other players (AI or otherwise) have their own agendas, attitudes, and economies and may surrender or betray you or do some of both. Imagine having another civilization that trades with you but is also paranoid, so you better not encroach on their territory. The win and lose conditions vary with the options chosen when starting the game. Also, we should offer both games that have a clear end and games that are open-ended. And if a game is won or lost, you can always convert it to open-ended so you can continue to play in the world; you just might not do so well if you lose and keep playing and keep getting ravaged by enemies, so there should also be the option to make enemies go passive.

The idea of capturing the keystone sounds like a reasonable approach to deciding when a civilization has been conquered. 

The Deepground concept has the double edged sword of not knowing anything about the game engine, so it's both unbiased as well as possibly suggesting things that we might choose not to do due to the difficulty.

We need to triage the best ideas that come together for a fun playable game. Some ideas should be left for subsequent versions. Some later versions may be upgrades and compatible, while others may be very different concepts. Also, the development progression is good too. We should be able to play the game somewhat when just the first epoch is more or less complete, even if we can't necessarily finish playing.

When merging these two game concepts, fun and challenging and balanced should be the driving factors. Sometimes you'll pick a concept from one game or the other. Sometime you'll do both. The idea isn't to mash the two game concepts together but rather to develop a third game concept that is better than either of these two. The are enough differently orthogonal elements to the designs that it might make sense to pick one from one source (e.g. building semantics) while picking something else from the other (e.g. combat mechanics.) Moreover, it's perfectly fine to pick one of the two while bottowing only a few ideas from the other.
